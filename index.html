Your Personalised Study Plan
🗂

Build a "Concepts Glossary" notebook

After each lecture, write down 5–10 key terms in your own words (PII, domain knowledge, data cleaning, aggregation, compliance). Writing forces understanding. Don't copy definitions — paraphrase them.

🔁

Use the Feynman Technique

Pick one concept per day (e.g. "why is aggregated data safer than PII?") and explain it out loud to yourself or a friend as if they're 10 years old. If you stumble, that's the gap you need to revisit.

🏭

Apply concepts to YOUR industry

The lecturer said this clearly — leverage your existing domain knowledge. Whatever field you came from, find a dataset in it. Your existing industry understanding is a competitive advantage over a pure fresh graduate.

⚠️

Practice asking "at what point does this become unethical?"

For every dataset or project you work on, force yourself to consider: What PII is here? Am I complying with POPIA? Who consented to what? This thinking habit separates average from excellent data practitioners.

📊

Never just read — always code along

For every concept covered (data cleaning, EDA, modelling), open a Jupyter notebook and implement it. Even toy examples with 20 rows build muscle memory faster than re-reading notes ever will.

🧪

Validate your data like a scientist

The lecturer said: "Never trust data." Internalise this. Every time you load a dataset, write a validation function before touching it. Check nulls, duplicates, data types, ranges, and outliers — always, even for small datasets.

🧭

Map the full workflow after every session

Draw the data pipeline (Collection → Cleaning → Exploration → Modelling → Presentation) and place every concept you learned that day into it. This builds a mental map that connects isolated ideas into a system.

🔍

Research South African data law (POPIA)

Spend 30 minutes reading the POPIA act summary. As a South African data practitioner, knowing this is not optional — it's a professional requirement and a career differentiator when working with local organisations.
